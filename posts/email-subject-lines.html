Email Subject Lines That Actually Get Opened

October 25, 2026

You can write the best email in the world, but if nobody opens it, it never existed. The subject line does one job: earn the open. Here are three rules that consistently work for small businesses.

Rule 1: Keep it under 50 characters

Most inboxes cut off subject lines at around 50 characters — and on mobile, even sooner. Short subjects get read in full; long ones get truncated mid-thought. Before you send, read your subject line on your phone. If it wraps awkwardly, cut it.

Rule 2: One email, one idea, one call to action

The fastest way to kill an email is to stuff three topics and four links into it. Each email should carry exactly one clear idea and end with exactly one call to action. When the reader finishes, they should know precisely what to do next — click, reply, or read.

Rule 3: Write like a person, not a newsletter

People open emails from people. "Quick question about your Tuesday" beats "OCTOBER NEWSLETTER — ISSUE #47" every time. Use a real sender name, keep the tone conversational, and avoid ALL CAPS and excessive exclamation marks — they trigger spam filters and human skepticism alike.

Bonus: test, don't guess

Send two subject lines to small segments of your list, then send the winner to everyone else. Even a list of 200 subscribers can run a meaningful A/B test. Over a year, the small wins from testing compound into dramatically higher open rates.

Email remains the highest-ROI channel most small businesses own — but only if the inbox door opens first. Master the subject line, and the rest of your email marketing finally gets a chance to work.
